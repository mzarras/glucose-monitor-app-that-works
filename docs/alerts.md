# Alerts

How Sensor raises alerts, why it does it this way, and where each safety rule is enforced.
Code: `Packages/AlertEngine`. Sounds: `Resources/Sounds` (generated by `tools/sounds/generate_sounds.py`).
Scope: HANDOFF §4.7, §5, §7.3 item 3; PROGRESS.md D4, D11, D17, D18, D22, D26, D27, D36, D37, D39, D41, D42, D43; WS4 safety review (B1, M1, minors) and delta review (B2 due repeats, partial rollback, alarm listing, stale Stop taps); D43 safety review (B1, M1, minors).

---

## 1. What makes an alert audible on iOS

| Mechanism | Silent switch on | Focus / DND | App must be running | Needs Apple approval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Notification, `.active` / `.timeSensitive` | **vibrates only** | Time Sensitive breaks through if the user allows it | no | Time Sensitive: capability only (we have it) |
| Notification, `.critical` + `criticalSoundNamed` | plays, at the volume we set | plays | no | **yes**: `com.apple.developer.usernotifications.critical-alerts`, granted per team |
| In-app audio, `AVAudioSession` category `.playback` | plays, at the **media** volume | plays | **yes** (awake) | no; needs `UIBackgroundModes` `audio` to activate from the background |
| AlarmKit alarm (iOS 26+) | plays (system alarm) | plays | no | no; needs user authorization + `NSAlarmKitUsageDescription`. Used for urgent low (D26, §4.4) |

Consequences, which decide the design (PROGRESS.md D17):

- Time Sensitive alone is **not** audible on silent.
- Critical Alerts are the only notification-based way through the switch, and Apple rarely grants
  them to build-from-source apps. We request them anyway (D4) and use them automatically if granted.
- The G7 wakes the app over BLE roughly every 5 minutes, and every reading-driven alert fires during
  such a wake. So in-app `.playback` audio at that moment is the primary ringer-off path.
- An alert that fires while **nothing** wakes the app (missed readings, i.e. signal loss) can only be
  a pending notification, which is vibrate-only on silent unless Critical Alerts are granted. See §4.2.
- On iOS 26+, with the user's permission, urgent low and its repeats are also system alarms, which
  ring on silent while the app is suspended (D26, §4.4).

## 3. Rules (HANDOFF §5)

Defaults and ranges live in `AlertLimits.swift`; settings clamp on every path (initialiser, setter, decoding).

| Alert (`AlertKind`) | Condition | Default | Configurable | Sound | Sound on silent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urgent low `urgentLow` | ≤ threshold, or LOW flag | 55 mg/dL, on | threshold 50–60, sound, repeat 2–5 min (never off, never > 5), snooze 1–30 (default 30) | Cascade | **always** (+ system alarm on iOS 26+, §4.4) |
| Urgent low soon `urgentLowSoon` | value + reported rate × 20 min ≤ urgent-low threshold | off | on/off, sound, snooze | Tide | on |
| Low `low` | ≤ threshold, or LOW flag | 70, on | on/off, threshold 60–100, sound, snooze (30) | Dusk | on |
| High `high` | ≥ threshold, or HIGH flag | 250, on, no delay | on/off, threshold 120–400, sound, snooze (120), delay 0–120 min | Summit | on |
| Falling fast `fallingFast` | reported rate ≤ −threshold | 3 mg/dL/min, off | on/off, threshold 2–5, sound, snooze (30) | Swoop | off |
| Rising fast `risingFast` | reported rate ≥ +threshold | 3 mg/dL/min, off | same | Swoop | off |
| Missed readings `missedReadings` | no valid reading for the window | 15 min, on | on/off, window 10–60, sound, snooze (30) | Sonar | on (effective only while awake, §4.2) |
| Sensor warm-up done `sensorWarmUpComplete` | one-off announcement when warm-up ends | on | on/off, sound, sound off | Doorbell | off |
| Sensor expiry reminders (D43, §3.1): 24 h `sensorExpiringIn24Hours`, 12 h `sensorExpiringIn12Hours`, 2 h `sensorExpiringIn2Hours`, grace period starts `sensorGracePeriod` | pending notification at expiry − 24 h / − 12 h / − 2 h / expiry | on; **24 h: no sound** | on/off each, sound, sound on/off (`playsSound`) | Doorbell | off |
| Sensor failed `sensorFailed` | `.failed(f)` with `f.raisesSensorAlert` only (D36, D37): sensor finished → "Replace the sensor"; pairing lost → "Pair the sensor again"; unclassified (`.other`) → "Sensor problem"; body is `f.message` | on | on/off, sound, snooze (120) | Warble | on |
| Sensor error / expired | `.error(code:description:)` / `.expired`; expired is also the last expiry reminder, pending for the grace period's end (D43, §3.1) | on | on/off each, sound, snooze (120) | Warble | on |
| Calibration needed `calibrationNeeded` | `.calibrationNeeded(code:)` or `.calibrationError(code:)` (D36); clears on any other settled state, e.g. active | on | on/off, sound, snooze (30) | Warble | on |
| Bluetooth unavailable `bluetoothUnavailable` | `.bluetoothUnavailable(.poweredOff/.unauthorized/.unsupported)` | on | on/off, sound, snooze (30) | Warble | on |
| Trio bridge `trioBridge` | `TrioBridgeStatus.writeFailing` (`.notConsumedSince` ≥ 15 min exists but is not fed: not detectable, D22) | on | on/off, sound, snooze (30) | Warble | on |
| Storage failing `storageFailing` | `StorageHealth.failing(since:)` for ≥ 5 min (D42): "History isn't being saved"; alerts and Trio still get readings | on | on/off, sound, snooze (30) | Warble | **off** |

Notes:

- **LOW/HIGH (S4).** `.belowRange` meets every at-or-below threshold and clears every at-or-above one;
  `.aboveRange` the reverse (`GlucoseConditions.atOrBelow/atOrAbove`). Neither is ever converted to 40/400;
  text writes "LOW (below sensor range)" / "HIGH (above sensor range)".
- **Urgent low soon (S1).** Uses only `GlucoseReading.trendRate` as reported. No rate → no projection
  (the condition is "unknown", nothing fires or clears). The projected number exists only inside the
  comparison; the notification shows the measured value, the reported rate, and the rule.
- **Supersession.** While urgent low is firing or snoozed, low and urgent-low-soon are cleared, so the
  two never sound together (as Loop does).
- **Hysteresis.** A glucose alert clears only 5 mg/dL past its threshold (rate alerts: 1 mg/dL/min).
- **Sensor states.** Only settled states (not paired, warming up, active, grace, expired, error,
  calibration needed/error, and failures that raise the sensor alert) affect sensor alerts. The
  5-minute connection cycle (searching → connecting → authenticating), Bluetooth states, and
  transient failures (D36: authentication failed, rejected after authentication, another app, clock
  mismatch, no glucose value, session length unknown, credentials unavailable, pairing incomplete)
  neither fire nor clear them; if readings stop for long, missed readings covers it.
  Bluetooth `.resetting` waits for the next state.
- **Missed readings** is suppressed while not paired and after a confirmed end of the session that
  another alert is announcing (D39 as corrected by D41): `.expired` while the sensor-expired alert is
  on, or a sensor-failed / session-stopped failure while the sensor-failed alert is on. That alert
  says so and stays until a new sensor; entering such a state removes the missed-readings
  notifications. Nothing else suppresses it — not an unsupported sensor, a lost pairing, a transient
  failure, or an end whose alert is turned off (then missed readings is the only alarm). G7Kit emits
  `.expired` only for a confirmed end; an unconfirmed report is `.error`. Leaving an expired/failed
  state does not restart the window: after a brief or false end report, missed readings stays due at
  the last reading + window. During warm-up it is suppressed only until warm-up
  ends by the clock (`endsAt`); throughout warm-up its notifications stay pending from `endsAt + window`,
  so it fires even if the phone is out of range when warm-up ends, or the source then reports only
  transient failures (D36 review B1). Its window restarts from `min(now, endsAt)` when the sensor
  leaves warm-up (a new sensor always warms up) and from now when it leaves "not paired"; a transient failure while "not paired"
  also ends that suppression, since a failure means a pairing exists. Calibration states do not
  suppress it.
- **Warm-up done** is announced once per sensor session. Session dates are derived from the phone
  clock at each connection (S3) and jitter by a second or so, so a date within an hour of the one
  already announced is the same session (review M1).
- **Quiet hours** (daily window, local time, wraps midnight): every alert except urgent low is posted
  `.passive` with no sound and no in-app sound, including low and missed readings (D27, exactly as
  §5). The decision is made for each pending notification's own fire time. Urgent low is never
  silenced (S7).

### 3.1 Sensor expiry reminders (D43)

Five notifications per sensor session, each on/off on its own (`AlertSettings.setEnabled(_:for:)`):

| Reminder | When | Title (on time) | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sensorExpiringIn24Hours` | expiry − 24 h | Sensor expires in 24 hours | The session ends tomorrow at 9:40 PM. A 12-hour grace period follows. |
| `sensorExpiringIn12Hours` | expiry − 12 h | Sensor expires in 12 hours | The session ends today at 9:40 PM. A 12-hour grace period follows. |
| `sensorExpiringIn2Hours` | expiry − 2 h | Sensor expires in 2 hours (at 9:40 PM) | Plan a sensor change. A 12-hour grace period follows. |
| `sensorGracePeriod` | expiry | Sensor session ended — 12 h grace period | Readings stop at 9:40 AM tomorrow. Replace the sensor before then. |
| `sensorExpired` | grace period's end | Sensor expired | The session has ended. No readings until a new sensor is paired. |

No 30-minute reminder and nothing else during the grace period. "today"/"tomorrow" are as seen at
delivery; the grace sentence is left out until the grace period's end is known.

- **Pre-scheduled.** Each is a pending notification (`sensor.alert.<kind>.slot0`, one-shot interval
  trigger), scheduled as soon as the session's dates are known, so it fires on time while the app is
  suspended or not running. The expiry comes from `.active(_, expiresAt:)`; the grace period's end from
  the session each reading arrives with (`SensorSession.gracePeriodEndsAt`) or from
  `.gracePeriod(endsAt:)`. The plan (`AlertEngineSnapshot.expiryPlan`) survives relaunches. A reminder
  posted by the engine itself (late, or because it was missing) uses the same identifier.
- **One notification per point.** Once a pending reminder's time has passed it counts as delivered,
  app running or not; the engine never posts it again. If a reminder's time passed with nothing
  pending (the session became known late, or the reminder was just turned on), only the latest one is
  posted at once, written for the time actually left ("Sensor expires in 1 h 15 min (at 9:40 PM)",
  "Sensor session ended — 9 h 20 min of grace period left"); earlier ones are out of date and skipped.
- **Never trusted blindly (D43 review B1).** A pending add can fail — typically because notifications
  were not allowed yet (skipped at onboarding, allowed later in iOS Settings while the app keeps
  running under BLE restoration) — or the system can drop it. So:
  - the executor reports every refused reminder add back; `AlertService` feeds it to the engine
    (`.notificationsNotScheduled`, automatic), which marks the reminder *not scheduled* instead of
    pending — it never counts as out;
  - refused or dropped reminders are added again when notification authorization changes (part of
    the delivery level the service tracks; the service then re-sends the settings, which rebuilds all
    pending notifications), on `.foreground` (the app sends it when it becomes active), at launch, and
    on a settings change;
  - when the current reminder's moment is reached while the app runs (the first input after it), the
    engine emits `.ensurePosted`: the executor looks the identifier up among pending and delivered
    notifications and posts it now, written for now, if it is missing; a refused one is posted
    directly. An earlier, out-of-date one is not checked;
  - a reminder the user opened or swiped away just after it came due counts as out without a check.
  A reminder the engine posts immediately (late) whose post fails is only logged: notifications are
  then off, and nothing can be shown until they are allowed.
- **Sensor expired** keeps its behaviour (condition alert, re-alerts every 120 min, D41 suppression of
  missed readings). When the sensor reports the end, its first notification is the reminder already
  pending or posted for the grace end: the executor checks it is there (`.ensurePosted`), posts it at
  once if it is missing or was refused — whatever "sound on silent" says — and plays the in-app sound
  when sound on silent is on. Quiet hours are decided for the moment of the report, not the grace end
  (review m1). An end reported more than a minute before that reminder is due removes it and posts
  now; if that report was brief and the grace period resumes, the reminder is scheduled again for the
  real end (review m5). With the alert off, nothing is pending for it.
  Known limit (review m6): opening, acknowledging or snoozing the posted "Sensor expired" *before* the
  sensor reports the end does not acknowledge the alert — there is no episode yet. At the report the
  alert starts and sounds as usual, and the notification is posted again if it is no longer in
  Notification Center.
- **Per session.** Dates within an hour of the plan's are the same session (jitter, review M1); a move
  of more than 2 minutes within that hour is adopted and the pending reminders are rewritten at the
  new dates (review m4; one due within 60 s is left alone). A new sensor warming up, no sensor, a
  sensor that must be replaced (`SensorFailure.requiresSensorReplacement`: failed, session stopped,
  unsupported; review M1), or other dates remove the pending reminders. A lost pairing, an
  unclassified failure, an error or a calibration state keeps them. If a sensor reported as needing
  replacement reports running again, the plan is rebuilt and the current reminder is posted late. A
  plan replacing another one without a warm-up in between (in practice the same sensor seen through a
  phone clock moved by more than an hour) skips the reminders already past rather than repeating them.
- **Switching.** Turning one off removes its pending notification and nothing else; turning it on
  schedules it, or posts it now if it is the current one. Other settings changes rewrite only the
  pending reminders whose notification changes (sound, sound on silent, sound on/off, quiet hours);
  all of them when the settings are re-sent unchanged (the service does that when the delivery level
  or the notification authorization changes). Opening or snoozing an announcement (`.dismiss`) removes
  only the notifications shown; its pending ones stay. A pending reminder due within 60 s is never
  removed or replaced (review m12).
- **Launch.** Every pending reminder is written again and every other reminder slot is cleared —
  including one whose reminder already went out or was taken over by the sensor-expired alert, so a
  stray left by a process that died before its cancel ran cannot post a second "Sensor expired"
  (review m2). One due within 60 s is left alone.
- **DEBUG replay (review m3).** An engine configured with `acceptsReplayReadings` uses slot 1
  (`sensor.alert.<kind>.slot1`), so its plan and launch never replace or remove the real reminders.
  Its condition alerts still share identifiers with production (`.cancel` of sensor expired removes
  both), as for every other alert in replay mode.
- **No sound (24 h).** `EventAlertSettings.playsSound` (`AlertSettings.playsSound(_:)` /
  `setPlaysSound(_:for:)`, announcements only; every other alert always plays its sound, urgent low
  by construction, S7). Off: an `.active` notification with no sound and no in-app sound, shown on the
  lock screen. Default off only for the 24-hour reminder. Settings stored before D43 have no
  `playsSound` and take the per-kind default, so the 24-hour reminder becomes silent on existing
  installs too until the user turns its sound on.
- **Upgrade.** A build installed mid-session does not repeat a 24 h / 2 h / grace announcement the
  previous build already made (`announcedSessionDates`).
- **Texts are written when scheduled**, with the time zone the engine has then; they are written again
  at every launch and `.foreground`. After a time-zone change while the app stays in the background,
  a pending reminder shows the old zone's clock time until then (it still fires at the right moment).

## 4. Delivery

### 4.1 Paths (`DeliveryPolicy.decide`, pure, fully tested)

| Situation | Notification | In-app `.playback` sound | System alarm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet hours (not urgent low) | `.passive`, no sound | no | no |
| Sound off (announcements only, D43: 24 h reminder by default) | `.active`, no sound | no | no |
| Sound on silent, Critical Alerts granted | `.critical`, `criticalSoundNamed(file, 1.0)` | no (the system plays it) | no (Critical wins, as in Loop) |
| **Urgent low**, alarms allowed (iOS 26+), Critical not granted — now or pending | `.timeSensitive`, `named(file)` | no (would clash) | **yes**, `alert-<sound>.caf` |
| Sound on silent, not granted, app awake | `.timeSensitive`, `named(file)` | **yes** (urgent low loops 30 s; others play once) | no |
| Sound on silent, not granted, pending (app may be suspended) | `.timeSensitive`, `named(file)` | not possible | no |
| Sound on silent off | `.timeSensitive`, `named(file)` (obeys the switch) | no | no |

The notification keeps its sound even when the app also plays it: with the ringer on but media
volume at zero, the notification sound is the one heard. With both up, both play (Loop accepts the
same). xDrip instead drops the notification sound when it plays in-app, which is silent in that case.

Urgent low is always treated as sound-on-silent and never silenced, in the engine **and** again in
`DeliveryPolicy` (`test_S7_urgentLowAlwaysGetsTheLoudestAvailablePath` enumerates every combination).

The in-app player vibrates at start and every 2 s while looping (Loop's pattern), so a phone with
media volume at zero still buzzes. A less important alert never interrupts a more important one's
sound (`AlertKind.priority`, urgent low first); cancelling an alert stops only its own sound.

### 4.2 The suspended-app gap (accepted, D17)

Missed readings and signal loss fire precisely when no BLE event wakes the app, so the in-app path
cannot run. The pending Time Sensitive notification is posted on time but, on a phone set to silent,
**vibrates without sound** (unless Critical Alerts are granted). Michael accepted this (D17). If the
app happens to be awake when it becomes due (foreground, or another wake-up), the engine emits
`playDueAlert` and the sound plays.

Urgent-low repeats between BLE wakes: on iOS 26+ with alarms allowed, each pending repeat is also a
system alarm and rings on silent (D26, §4.4; unproven on hardware). Otherwise they have the same
property as missed readings, and the next reading (≤ 5 min) wakes the app and plays the sound again.

No keep-alive technique is built (e.g. a silent looping audio session): that was ruled out.

### 4.3 Volume

`.playback` audio follows the media volume. iOS has no public API to set it. Loop and the Trio
worktree push it up by moving the hidden `UISlider` inside an `MPVolumeView`; by Loop's own comment
this no-ops when the app is backgrounded (the 3 a.m. case), and it depends on a private view
hierarchy. Not proven for our case, so not built. Instead `AlertDeliveryStatus.problems` reports
`.alarmVolumeMuted` (≤ 0.001) and `.alarmVolumeLow` (< 0.25) from `AVAudioSession.outputVolume`
for the Settings screen (S5). Critical Alerts, if granted, play at the volume we set regardless.

### 4.4 System alarms for urgent low (D26)

On iOS 26+ (deployment target stays 17.0), once the user allows alarms, urgent low rings as an
AlarmKit system alarm: on silent, in Focus, while the app is suspended, without the Critical Alerts
entitlement. Only urgent low uses alarms; every other alert is unchanged.

- **Policy.** `DeliveryPolicy` returns `systemAlarmSoundFileName` for urgent low when alarms are
  authorized and Critical Alerts are not granted (Critical wins, as in Loop `AlertManager.swift:451-457`).
  The Time Sensitive notification is still posted (visual, Snooze/Acknowledge, fallback sound); the
  in-app sound is not played (it would clash with the alarm).
- **Immediate alarm.** Scheduled 2 s ahead: a fixed alarm at or before now never rings (Loop
  `CriticalAlertAlarmScheduler.swift:73-76`, commit `25f79bd`).
- **Repeats while suspended.** Loop schedules only the immediate alarm, while the app is awake. Here
  every pending urgent-low repeat (the same slots as the pending notifications, 60 minutes ahead) is
  also an alarm, so the repeats ring while the app sleeps. **This is unproven on hardware**: whether
  a pre-scheduled `.fixed` alarm rings reliably, how a ringing alarm interacts with the next one, and
  whether AlarmKit accepts 31 alarms at once (the immediate one plus 30 repeats at the 2-minute
  floor) — `AlarmManager.AlarmError.maximumLimitReached` exists, its value is not documented. These
  are HANDOFF §11 checks (§10 below).
- **IDs.** Slot 0 is the immediate alarm; each pending repeat has the slot `sequence mod 128`, where
  the sequence number counts every repeat the engine has scheduled (persisted in the snapshot with
  its fire date, `RepeatSeries`). The same slot names its notification. IDs therefore never depend
  on a repeat's position in a list, the whole set (`SystemAlarmIDs.all`) is known, and every ID is
  re-derivable after a relaunch. A new repeat never takes a slot held by any repeat the engine still
  remembers (at most ~60 of 128: an hour past + an hour ahead at the 2-minute floor): rapid settings
  changes can burn through the 128 sequence numbers in seconds, and a wrap onto a protected repeat
  would cancel it (second delta review; `test_S7_idReuseNeverTouchesALivePendingRepeat`). If no
  slot were free (it cannot happen within the bounds above), fewer repeats are planned, never a live
  one disturbed, and it is loud: logged, and `DeliveryProblem.repeatsNotAllScheduled` on the
  Settings screen (`test_S5_shortfallIsLoggedAndSurfacedThenClears`).
- **A due or ringing repeat is never touched (delta review B2).** A wake-up up to 60 s before a repeat
  counts as that repeat, so rescheduling "from the next slot" used to cancel the repeat due 30 s later
  or stop the one ringing. Now planning is a diff against the repeat grid: entries due within 60 s
  (`protectedLeadTime`) or already past are never cancelled or replaced; only later off-grid entries
  are cancelled by slot and missing grid points added. Rolling forward on the same grid only adds the
  tail. The same applies to settings changes, delivery-level rebuilds and the launch reconcile, and to
  the missed-readings notifications. Belt and braces: when a repeat is due and its alarm is not in the
  system's list (removed, never scheduled, or the list is unreadable), the app plays the in-app sound.
  `test_S7_wakeNearARepeatNeverCancelsTheDueAlarm` and `test_S7_ringingRepeatIsNeverStoppedByReschedule`
  use fakes that fail the test the moment a due or ringing alarm or notification is removed; with the
  old executor they fail 42 assertions.
- **Clock set back while a repeat rings.** The pending alarms are for wall-clock times that no longer
  make sense and are re-planned from now — except the ones due by real time (+60 s, from the
  monotonic clock): those are ringing or about to, stay untouched, and are re-dated in the engine's
  record so later plans keep protecting them (`test_rollbackDoesNotStopTheRingingAlarm`). One that
  was about to ring may ring late by the size of the change; the new grid rings on time regardless.
- **Cancel.** Acknowledge, snooze, recovery, supersession, and turning the alert off all emit
  `.cancel(.urgentLow)`: the executor stops and cancels **every** alarm the app has. `cancelAll`
  cancels every known ID first — this works even when AlarmKit cannot list alarms (it throws once
  the user revokes permission) — then anything else listed; a listing failure is surfaced as
  `.urgentLowAlarmFailed` (S5).
- **Launch reconcile.** `AlertService.start()` sends `.launched`: if urgent low is not firing,
  leftover pending notifications and alarms are removed; if it is firing, they are rebuilt from the
  restored state.
- **Failure.** Scheduling is async and can throw. The immediate alarm failing falls back at once to
  the D17 path (notification sound + in-app sound); a failed repeat leaves its pending notification.
  Either way `AlertDeliveryStatus.problems` gets `.urgentLowAlarmFailed(error)` until a later attempt
  succeeds in full (S5).
- **Stop.** The alarm's Stop button runs the app's `StopUrgentLowAlarmIntent`, which calls
  `UrgentLowAlarmIntentBridge.shared.stopTapped()` → `.acknowledge(.urgentLow)` → snooze for the
  urgent-low snooze time, ≤ 30 minutes (S7), and every alarm is cancelled. Taps that arrive before
  the service is connected are queued, and dropped if older than 60 s when it connects, so a late
  delivery cannot acknowledge a newer episode. A Snooze button, if added, goes through
  `snoozeTapped(minutes:)` and is clamped by the engine to ≤ 30.
- **Permission.** `SystemAlarmAuthorization` (`unavailable` below iOS 26, `notDetermined`, `denied`,
  `authorized`) is `AlertDeliveryStatus.urgentLowAlarm`, for the Settings "Urgent low alarm" row;
  `notDetermined` and `denied` are `DeliveryProblem`s. Granting it rebuilds the pending repeats as alarms.
- **Sound.** AlarmKit plays `alert-<sound>.caf` (IMA4), the same sound as the `.wav`: Loop verified on
  hardware that AlarmKit plays bundled IMA4 `.caf` files by name (PR #2520); WAV is unverified there.
  `test_alarmVariantsAreIMA4CAFsThatDecodeToTheSameSound` decodes each `.caf` with Apple's decoder.

### 4.5 What needs the Apple entitlement

Only `.critical` delivery. Without it: authorization still includes `.criticalAlert` (harmless),
`criticalAlertSetting` stays `.notSupported`, and the policy falls back to Time Sensitive + in-app
sound. To enable: Apple grants the entitlement to the team, then set `SENSOR_APP_ENTITLEMENTS =
App/Sensor+CriticalAlerts.entitlements` (D4). The app switches automatically at the next permission
check (`AlertService.refreshDeliveryStatus`), and pending notifications are rebuilt for the new level.

### 4.6 Notifications

- Identifiers: `sensor.alert.<kind>` (immediate; a new one replaces the old) and
  `sensor.alert.<kind>.slot<0…127>` (pending; slot = sequence number mod 128, see §4.4). Urgent low keeps
  60 minutes of repeats pending (12 at the default 5 minutes, 30 at the 2-minute floor); missed readings
  keeps 12 (≥ 2 hours); each sensor expiry reminder is `sensor.alert.<kind>.slot0` (D43; slot1 in DEBUG replay). At most 47
  future pending (30 + 12 + 5), under iOS's 64. Pending notifications use one-shot
  interval triggers, never past times, and one due within 60 s is never removed or replaced.
- Categories: `sensor.alert.category.snoozable` (Acknowledge, Snooze) and
  `sensor.alert.category.informational` (announcements and expiry reminders; the sensor-expired one is
  snoozable); both `.customDismissAction`.
- Responses (`AlertNotificationResponse`): open or Acknowledge → acknowledge; Snooze → snooze for the
  duration carried in the notification (urgent low clamped to 30); swipe → stop the sound only (an
  accidental swipe must not end urgent-low repeats).

## 5. State machine

One per condition alert (`AlertEpisode`):

```
          condition met                      delay elapsed (high only)
 armed ────────────────▶ [delaying] ───────────────────────────────▶ firing
   ▲  ◀── clear ──────────┘                                           │   │
   │                                                     acknowledge /│   │ repeat slot or
   │                                                     snooze       ▼   │ re-alert interval
   │                                                              snoozed │ (sounds again)
   │                                                                │     │
   │               snooze over and condition still met ─────────────┘─▶ firing
   │
   └──────────── clear (recovered past the margin, turned off, superseded) ── from firing / snoozed
                 = "cleared": removes delivered + pending notifications, stops the sound
```

- **Condition** is `met`, `holding` or `clear`. `holding` means unknown or in the hysteresis band:
  no reading is current (older than 6 minutes, S1), no reported rate (urgent low soon, rates), a
  transient sensor state, Bluetooth resetting, or Trio not consuming for < 15 min. Holding never fires
  and never clears.
- **Delay (high)**: counts from the first reading at or above the threshold. A reading in the band or
  missing data does not restart it; only a recovered reading does (Loop's `decideAndUpdate`). It then
  fires on the first current reading at or above the threshold once the delay has passed.
- **Fire**: posts the notification now. Urgent low also schedules 60 minutes of repeats (notifications, and alarms on iOS 26+).
  Sensor expired: if its expiry reminder is pending (due within 60 s) or was posted, the executor checks
  it is there and posts it only if not, then plays the sound (`.ensurePosted`); one pending later is
  removed and the alert posts now (D43, §3.1).
- **Urgent-low repeats**: every `repeatMinutes` (5) from the first sounding until acknowledged, snoozed
  or cleared — **including while the latest value is stale or in the band**. The alert was raised on a
  real reading and nobody acknowledged it; the pending notifications repeat it while the app sleeps,
  and each wake adds the ringer-off sound (`playDueAlert`) and rolls the pending ones forward. A wake up
  to 60 s before a slot counts as that slot (the G7 wakes about every 5 minutes, anchored at the wake
  that fired the alert, so the next wake often lands just before the slot). Repeat
  text always states the measured value and its time ("Not acknowledged. Last reading 49 mg/dL at
  12:05 PM.") so an old value is never presented as current (S1).
- **Other alerts** re-alert at their snooze interval while unacknowledged, but only when a *current*
  reading (or state) still meets the condition.
- **Acknowledge** = snooze for the alert's snooze minutes. **Snooze** applies only to a firing or
  snoozed alert: there is no pre-emptive snooze, so every new episode sounds. Both remove the alert's
  notifications and stop its sound.
- **Snooze expiry** re-fires only on a current reading/state that meets the condition. A stale value
  never re-fires anything (S1); missed readings covers the gap.
- **Clear** (recovery, supersession, alert turned off) re-arms and removes everything. A snooze never
  survives an episode.
- **Missed readings** has its own machine (`AlertEngine+MissedReadings.swift`): armed with its
  notifications pending from `last valid reading + window`; `firing` from that time; repeats every
  window; acknowledge reschedules from the snooze end; any valid reading (backfill included) clears it
  and reschedules. Refused readings (S2/S6) change nothing, including this clock.
- **Readings.** Glucose alerts evaluate only the newest *live, non-backfilled* reading, and only while
  it is ≤ 6 minutes old. A backfilled reading never fires, clears or re-decides a glucose alert (Loop's
  G7 bug, and the orchestrator's rule); it does advance the missed-readings clock. A live reading older
  than one already seen is ignored.
- **Relaunch.** `AlertEngineSnapshot` (Codable) holds every episode and fact; the app persists it
  after each input and restores it, so acknowledgements and snoozes survive a background relaunch.
  Decoding is field by field: a new, missing or unreadable field takes its default and an unreadable
  episode is dropped, so a format change never resets the engine. `.launched` reconciles what the
  previous process left pending.
- **Clock faults (review B1, delta major).** Before every input, and on restore, the engine checks the
  wall clock against where it should be: the last input's `now` plus the real time elapsed on a
  monotonic clock that keeps counting in sleep and is unaffected by time adjustments
  (`AlertClock.uptime`: Darwin `CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW`, tagged with `kern.bootsessionuuid` so two
  readings are compared only within one known boot; after a reboot, if the boot session cannot be
  read, or without the clock, the check is "earlier than the latest `now` seen"). More than 60 s behind is a rollback — including one smaller than the time already elapsed
  (24 minutes back, 25 minutes into a snooze), even with no input in between. On a rollback: the
  **urgent-low snooze ends at once** (S7: real elapsed time since the snooze began cannot be trusted),
  other snoozes keep what was left of them, firing/delay timers and the missed-readings window
  restart from now, and the urgent-low repeats (whose alarms are set for wall-clock times) are planned
  again from now. Independently, anything remembered later than `now + 60 s` is repaired: a
  future-dated latest reading is dropped (it would hide every real reading), and so on.
  `currentReading` refuses a reading more than 60 s in the future. A clock moving forward needs no
  repair: readings go stale (not evaluated), missed readings fires, snoozes end early, and a firing
  urgent low repeats once and reschedules.

## 7. Other safety rules

- **S1**: glucose alerts only on the current live reading (`AlertEngine.currentReading`, `accept`);
  urgent low soon only with a reported rate; text always carries the measurement time; no projected
  value shown; a reading dated in the future is not current and is dropped. Tests `test_S1_*` in
  `GlucoseAlertTests`, `AlertConditionsTests`, `AlertTextTests`, `ClockFaultTests`, including
  `test_S1_staleBackfillDoesNotFireGlucoseAlert`, `test_S1_futureDatedReadingIsNotCurrentAfterClockRollback`,
  `test_S1_urgentLowFiresAfterClockRollback`.
- **S2/S6**: every reading goes through `LiveReadingGate` inside `AlertEngine.handle`; a refused
  reading returns only `.readingRejected` and changes nothing. Release builds call only
  `validate` (sensor only); `validateAllowingReplay` is compiled only under `#if DEBUG` and used only
  when `AlertEngineConfiguration.acceptsReplayReadings` (a DEBUG-only property) is on (D11). Tests
  `test_S6_*`, `test_S2_implausibleReadingsNeitherFireNorClear`; the Release variant
  `test_S6_releaseBuildRefusesReplayReadings` runs under `swift test -c release -Xswiftc -enable-testing`.
- **S4**: `GlucoseConditions`, `AlertText.valueText`; `test_S4_*`.
- **S5**: missed readings, Bluetooth, sensor failed/error/expired, Trio bridge are alerts; denied
  notifications, muted/low media volume, alarms not allowed and alarm scheduling failures are
  `DeliveryProblem`s for Settings; missed readings survives a clock rollback. `test_S5_*`, including
  `test_S5_alarmSchedulingFailureFallsBackAndSurfaces`, `test_S5_missedReadingsSurvivesClockRollback`.
  Permissions (notifications, Critical, alarms) are re-read on every input, so the wake that delivers
  an urgent low uses the current delivery level.

## 10. On-device checks (HANDOFF §11)

The host tests prove the decisions; these need the phone:

1. Ringer off, app backgrounded, BLE wake with an urgent low: the in-app sound plays (D17 path, AlarmKit off).
2. iOS 26+, alarms allowed: urgent low rings as a system alarm playing Cascade; **the pre-scheduled
   repeat alarms ring 5 and 10 minutes later with the app suspended** (unproven: Loop only schedules
   immediate alarms); Stop acknowledges (the next repeat does not ring; notifications are removed).
3. How many alarms AlarmKit accepts: 31 at the 2-minute repeat floor (immediate + 30 repeats), 13 at
   the default 5 minutes; `maximumLimitReached` is unverified. A refusal shows the "urgent low alarm
   failed" problem in Settings.
3a. A BLE wake 30 s before, and 3 s after, a repeat: the alarm still rings / keeps ringing (the host
   tests prove the app does not touch it; the phone proves AlarmKit agrees).
3b. Revoke alarms in Settings while a repeat is pending: after re-allowing, nothing stale rings.
4. A phone call during the urgent-low loop: the sound resumes when the call ends.
5. Set the clock back and forward by hand with an urgent low in progress (review B1 scenarios), and
   change the time by hand **during an urgent-low snooze** (e.g. back 24 minutes, 25 minutes in): urgent
   low must sound again within 30 real minutes. Also confirm `CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW` keeps counting
   through a locked, sleeping phone, and that `kern.bootsessionuuid` is readable by the app on the
   phone (log `SystemAlertClock().uptime?.boot` once): if it is not, the partial-rollback check
   without an intervening input is inactive and only the latest-wall-clock fallback applies.
6. Media volume at zero: Settings shows the warning; the notification sound still plays with the ringer on.
7. Expiry reminders (D43), e.g. with a sensor near its end or a replay session: each arrives on time
   with the app suspended and after a force-quit; the 24-hour one appears on the lock screen with no
   sound or vibration; opening the app afterwards posts nothing a second time; the sensor reporting
   the end after the "Sensor expired" reminder plays its sound without a second banner.
8. Skip notifications at onboarding, pair a sensor, then allow notifications in iOS Settings without
   killing the app: the reminders are pending again (log "schedule …"), and a reminder whose add was
   refused is posted when its moment comes. Also check that `pendingNotificationRequests` /
   `deliveredNotifications` see the reminders (the `.ensurePosted` check relies on them).
